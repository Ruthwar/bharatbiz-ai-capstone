# Case Note - Split Delivery at Month End

SIMULATION ONLY. Synthetic case note; all identifiers are fictional.

## 1. What happened

On 29 September a supplier wrote: "Tea 1 kg pack: 60% can go tomorrow, remaining 40% next week." The message contained two quantities and two dates for a single order line. The intake system acknowledged receipt and routed the item to triage with the reason that partial dispatch is not modelled.

## 2. What the desk did

1. Recorded the two quantities as stated, without converting percentages into units.
2. Called the supplier to confirm the unit counts, because a percentage of an unstated order quantity is not a quantity.
3. Split the line manually: confirmed units for the next day, unconfirmed units for the following week.
4. Planned each line separately. The next-day line covered two retailer orders; the following-week line covered one.
5. Left the retailer message to a human; no arrival date was promised.

## 3. What would have gone wrong with averaging

If the planner had averaged the two dates, it would have produced a single arrival date that neither part of the delivery would meet. The review card would then have shown a plan that looked feasible and was not. The 60/40 split also cannot be turned into units without the order quantity, which the message did not state.

## 4. Outcome

All three retailer orders were covered, one of them late. The supplier's second tranche arrived two days after the stated week, and the desk re-planned the affected order on the day.

## 5. Lesson

A percentage is not a quantity. A split is two facts, not one average. Both need a human until the planner can hold two arrival dates for one line.

## 6. Follow-up

SOP-SHT-003 was updated to require unit-level confirmation before any split is planned. The intake vocabulary still has no partial-dispatch intent; this remains a known gap.
