# Case Note - Cooking Oil Delay and the 28 Late Litres

SIMULATION ONLY. Synthetic case note; all identifiers are fictional.

## 1. What happened

A supplier message reported a two-day delay for Cooking oil 1 L pouch (SKU-003) and stated that the revised date was not confirmed. Open retailer orders due inside the horizon requested 102 litres. Reserved stock was excluded, leaving 56 usable litres, so 46 litres had to be bought.

## 2. What the planner compared

| Option | Covers | Late units | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stock only | 56 of 102 | 0 | 46 units unmet |
| SUP-005 (delayed supplier) | 46 bought | 28 | Same late count, later arrival |
| SUP-002 | 46 bought | 28 | Earlier arrival |
| SUP-003 | 46 bought | 28 | Lower unit cost, later arrival |

Every supplier option tied on coverage and on 28 late units. The tie was broken by earliest scenario arrival, so SUP-002 won and cost slightly more than the cheapest option. The review card stated the extra rupees paid and that they bought an earlier arrival, not fewer late units.

## 3. Why 28 late units were accepted

No plan could avoid the late units: the delayed incoming stock was the only supply that could meet the earliest requested dates, and it was late. The choice was between plans that all delivered 28 units late. Approving the plan did not mean the late units were acceptable to the retailer; it meant the proposal was the best available and the late units were visible.

## 4. What went well

- The unconfirmed revised date stayed an unknown. Nobody invented a date from "two days".
- Stock-only was shown with its 46-unit gap rather than being hidden.
- Verification passed before review, so the reviewer checked judgement, not arithmetic.

## 5. What to improve

The difference between plans was a day or two of arrival for a small rupee difference. The review card expresses this clearly, but a reviewer still has to read the trade-off line to see it. A future version could sort alternatives by the ranking key so the ordering is self-evident.

## 6. Lesson

When coverage and lateness tie, the decision is about time, not price. Saying "we chose the cheapest" here would have been false.
