# Market-Signal Corroboration Policy

SIMULATION ONLY. Synthetic policy for an academic capstone. Market signals are indicators, not confirmed demand or supply facts.

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how reports such as retailer comments, supplier messages, local events, price observations and public information are corroborated before they influence a simulation or review.

## 2. Rules

**R1 - Label the signal.** Record source, observation date, geography, SKU or category, direction, confidence and exact wording where available.

**R2 - One source is a lead.** A single unverified signal must not change orders, stock, price, cash, supplier capacity or customer commitments.

**R3 - Corroborate independently.** A corroborating source must be independent in origin and should support the same direction, time window and market scope.

**R4 - Do not double count.** Copies, forwarded messages and sources that repeat one original claim count as one signal.

**R5 - Separate observation from inference.** “Retailer reports a shortage” is an observation; “demand will rise 20%” is an assumption and must be labelled as such.

**R6 - Time-box signals.** A signal has an observation date and expiry or review date. Stale signals are not silently carried forward.

**R7 - Human approval.** A corroborated signal may inform a labelled demand scenario, but a human must approve any operational recommendation.

## 3. Evidence quality

High-quality evidence is attributable, dated, specific and independently corroborated. Conflicting signals remain conflicting; they are not averaged to manufacture certainty.

## 4. Audit

The record keeps the source list, relationship between sources, corroboration decision, scenario link and reviewer. The original signal is never replaced by the conclusion.
