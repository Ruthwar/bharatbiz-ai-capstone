# Retailer Service Continuity Policy

SIMULATION ONLY. This is a synthetic policy written for an academic capstone. It is not a real distributor policy. All retailers, products and amounts are fictional.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy guides advisory fulfilment recommendations when available supply cannot meet every accepted retailer order on time. It supports service continuity while keeping allocations and commitments reviewable by a human.

## 2. Rules

**R1 - Protect accepted order commitments.** When comparing eligible plans, consider the number of accepted order units that would be late or unmet and the requested delivery dates recorded in the versioned order state. Do not create or change an order promise from an unverified message.

**R2 - Use recorded service priorities only.** A retailer service tier or order priority may influence which late or unmet orders matter most only when that priority is present in the approved business state and its definition is available. Do not infer priority from a retailer's tone, relationship, location or message frequency.

**R3 - Prefer a service improvement when it is supported by the case.** If an eligible plan materially reduces lateness or unmet demand for accepted orders, that improvement is a valid reason to prefer it over a less expensive plan. State which verified orders and outcomes support the service benefit.

**R4 - Service does not bypass controls.** A service concern cannot make an infeasible supplier offer, unaffordable plan, unverified allocation, unauthorized substitution or unsafe product eligible. Apply POL-DLY-001, POL-CSH-002, POL-LIQ-007 and POL-SUB-003 first.

**R5 - Escalate unsupported urgency.** If urgency is asserted in a message but cannot be tied to a verified order, delivery date or approved service priority, show it as an unverified report. Do not treat it as a confirmed service obligation.

## 3. Review output

For each recommended service improvement, the review card identifies the affected accepted orders, their recorded due dates, and the verified change in late or unmet units. The card labels scenario arrival dates as estimates and does not promise a delivery date.

## 4. Audit

The recommendation records the order-state version and the service outcomes supplied to the decision process. A change to orders, due dates or service-priority data requires a fresh plan and review.
