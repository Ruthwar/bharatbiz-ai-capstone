# Case Note - Retailer Reporting a Supplier Delay

SIMULATION ONLY. Synthetic case note; all identifiers are fictional.

## 1. What happened

A retailer wrote: "SUP-005 has delayed my oil order by two days; when will it come?" The message named a supplier, a product and a delay. A rules-only extractor reading keywords would have produced a valid supplier-delay event.

## 2. Why it was not planned

The identity gate rejected it. The sender was a retailer account, not a supplier account, and a retailer cannot report a supplier's dispatch delay. Planning from a retailer's account of a supplier's schedule would mean building a purchase plan on second-hand information about a party the sender does not represent.

## 3. What the system did

- Sent the receipt acknowledgement. The template asserted no date.
- Routed to human triage with reason `authorization_failed`.
- Recorded the message text and the facts that were read, without creating a plan.

## 4. What the desk did

The desk checked the supplier's own messages. There was no delay notice from SUP-005 that day. The retailer's order was due the following day and arrived on time. The retailer had mistaken a different shop's delivery for their own.

## 5. Why the gate is not just bureaucracy

This case would have produced a plausible, well-formatted, wrong plan: a purchase from a second supplier to cover a delay that did not exist. The cost would have been real money and a damaged supplier relationship. The gate cost one triage item.

## 6. Lesson

Evidence about a party must come from that party, or from a system that is authoritative for them. A confident message from the wrong party is still the wrong evidence.
