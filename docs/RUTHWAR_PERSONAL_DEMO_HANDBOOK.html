4. All nine scenarios I can demonstrate

Use Inbox → Demo examples → Run full scenario or select the fixture in Scenario lab. Full-scenario mode includes the fixture’s demand, cash, delay, stock, duplicate-report, or observation overlays. Editable intake processes only the visible message.

SCN-001 · Normal day stock only

Normal day planning for Biscuits family carton.

Expected: stock-only; 70/70 pieces fulfilled; zero late and zero unmet; verification valid.

Why show it: proves the planner does not procure unnecessarily when stock is sufficient.

SCN-002 · Demand spike procurement

Demand spike: open orders for Biscuits small carton are expected to rise by 60 percent.

Expected: SUP-001; 112/112 pieces fulfilled; 47 procured; zero late and zero unmet.

Why show it: demonstrates an explicit scenario assumption and per-order ceiling rounding.

SCN-003 · Supplier delay main demo

Sri Venkateswara FMCG Supply reports a two-day delay for Cooking oil 1 L pouch; revised date is not confirmed.

Expected: SUP-002; buy 46 litre units for ₹4,876.46; 102/102 fulfilled; 28 late; zero unmet; arrival 3 Nov; verification valid.

Why show it: the complete vertical slice—evidence, unknowns, alternatives, cash, ranking, verification, citations, and human review.

SCN-004 · Limited cash + delayed retailer payment counterfactual

Limited cash for Cooking oil 1 L pouch: RET-003 payment of 30000 rupees expected today is now delayed until 2026-11-08.

Expected: actual stock-only view fulfils 68/102 litre units and leaves 34 unmet. The on-time-receipt counterfactual selects SUP-005.

Why show it: separates the real scenario from a clearly labelled “what if”; it does not hide a cash constraint.

SCN-005 · Stock discrepancy unverified count

Stock discrepancy for Biscuits small carton: physical count is 48 pieces against book stock of 63 pieces.

Expected: preserve book stock 63 beside physical count 48; scenario usable stock 50; SUP-001 procures 19; zero unmet.

Why show it: an unverified physical count does not silently overwrite book inventory.

SCN-006 · Retailer delivery complaint clarification

My Cooking oil 1 L pouch delivery has not arrived. Please check it.

Expected: two orders match; ask for the order reference; status needs clarification; no allocation plan is created.

Why show it: asking a precise question is safer than choosing an order based on probability.

SCN-007 · Competitor observation market signal

Competitor ValueMart is offering 20 percent off Rice 5 kg bag at a nearby shop in Kukatpally.

Expected full fixture: two unique reports from two distinct reporters; corroborated but unverified; no automatic competitive action.

Why show it: corroboration counts independent reporters, not repeated message volume.

SCN-008 · Combined disruption composed overlays

Combined disruption for Cooking oil 1 L pouch: demand up 60 percent, SUP-005 delayed two days, and retailer payment delayed.

Expected: stock-only actual view; 56/166 litre units fulfilled; 110 unmet; on-time-receipt counterfactual selects SUP-005 for 110 units.

Why show it: multiple disruptions are composed once through explicit overlays rather than hardcoded into the planner.

SCN-009 · Smaller-pack request market signal

Customers are requesting Cooking oil 500 ml pouch. Can you supply this smaller pack?

Expected full fixture: nine reported requests from three retailers; no catalogue match; unverified signal; no assortment change.

Why show it: demand evidence can be useful without automatically creating a SKU or changing the assortment.

Important distinction: SCN-007’s second reporter and SCN-009’s nine requests exist in the full fixture, not in the single visible sentence. Use Run full scenario when stating those aggregate outcomes.

What the LLM does—and does not do

LLM responsibility

  • Classify the message intent.
  • Extract stated entities into a strict structure.
  • Provide exact evidence spans.
  • Leave unstated fields null.

Not an LLM responsibility

  • No inventory or order arithmetic.
  • No supplier ranking or cash calculation.
  • No fabricated dates, quantities, SKUs, or PO references.
  • No autonomous approval or operational execution.

Validation before planning

  1. The model output must match the expected schema.
  2. Every evidence span must occur verbatim in the original message.
  3. Product and business references must agree with the catalogue/state.
  4. Unknown or ambiguous information stays unknown or triggers clarification.
  5. Model errors and validation violations fall back to deterministic rules and remain visible in evaluation.
Best explanation: “The model proposes structured facts. It does not get trusted merely because its JSON looks fluent. Deterministic gates decide whether that structure is safe enough to enter the workflow.”

(unmet units, late units, arrival date, total cost, candidate ID)

Evidence, not decoration

7. RAG explained and measured

RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. In this project its clearest role is retrieval of approved policy and procedure passages for review. The retriever does not invent inventory numbers and does not choose the supplier.

Simple answer: “When the reviewer asks why a rule applies, the system shows the relevant document section, exact quote, document ID, effective date, and licence. RAG supplies checkable context; business state supplies operational numbers.”

What is inside the corpus?

  • 30 documents and 186 sections: 27 synthetic policies, SOPs, supplier terms, and case notes, plus three cited public regulatory extracts.
  • Sources, licences, effective dates, and retrieval provenance are recorded in docs/RAG_CORPUS_SOURCES.md.
  • The current curated corpus is approximately 89.1 KiB. Quality and relevance matter more than file size.
  • The review card shows up to three relevant sections. SCN-003 citation coverage includes POL-DLY-001, POL-CSH-002, and CASE-2026-014.

Why SQLite FTS5 + BM25 instead of embeddings?

The corpus is small, curated, mostly English, and contains exact policy terms. BM25 gives deterministic, local, millisecond-scale retrieval with no model download or network call. It was measured against a simple whole-document term-frequency baseline rather than assumed to be better.

Citation checks

  • 3/3 displayed references contain a non-empty verbatim quote.
  • 3/3 contain document, effective-date, and licence provenance.
  • 3/3 are effective on the scenario date, 1 Nov 2026.
  • SCN-003 citation coverage explicitly uses rules extraction so this retrieval check does not accidentally call the LLM.
Known RAG limitation: primary@1 is not 100%. Short sections and repeated policy wording can put an acceptable SOP or terms sheet above the canonical policy. Document-level scoring, a reranker, or multilingual embeddings are future improvements—not features I should claim are already present.

10. Safety, limitations, and honest scope

Implemented

  • Nine repeatable scenario fixtures.
  • Optional validated Qwen extraction with rules fallback.
  • Deterministic planning, dated cash, exclusions, and counterfactuals.
  • Independent plan verification.
  • Curated BM25 retrieval with citations.
  • Durable retail cases, drafts, interactions, and observations.
  • RAG and extraction evaluation dashboards.
  • 114 passing tests and nine passing scenario evaluations in the verified build.

Not claimed

  • No live ERP, WhatsApp, email, or supplier-portal integration.
  • No automatic purchase, payment, pricing, credit, stock, or assortment action.
  • No substantive automatic retailer/supplier message.
  • No production authentication, tenant isolation, or complete audit deployment.
  • No proof of accuracy on real business messages.
  • No vector database or multilingual embedding retriever.
  • No general mathematical optimizer or multi-supplier transport optimization.
  • No commercial production readiness.

Important technical limitations

  • All business data and evaluation records shown here are synthetic.
  • The 40 retrieval questions are self-written and the 12 extraction records are a very small held-out set.
  • The model’s strict extraction results are weak; more data, error analysis, and controlled iteration are needed on separate tuning examples.
  • BM25 can rank overlapping wording imperfectly and is limited for semantic multilingual retrieval.
  • Scenario ranking is a fixed lexicographic policy, not a universally optimal business objective.
  • Supplier payment terms, substitutions, partial shipments, multi-supplier splits, and transport constraints are not modelled.
  • Evaluation job history is in memory and disappears on API restart; durable retail/review records are separate.

Privacy and safety wording

Say this: “The demo uses fictional synthetic data. Real pilot data would require permission, minimization, de-identification, restricted access, retention rules, and authorization before any external model processing. Secrets remain in a gitignored environment file and are never shown or committed.”
Approval wording: “Approval changes only demo/review state. It never executes an impactful operation. An approved-action integration would require separate credentials, authorization, idempotency, audit, and explicit action contracts.”

Mentor questions

11. FAQs and answers I can reuse

Project purpose and design

1. What problem does BharatBiz AI solve?
It helps an FMCG distributor turn messy operational messages into evidence-linked events and reviewable recommendations. Examples include supplier delays, demand spikes, limited cash, stock discrepancies, delivery complaints, and market observations. It reduces manual checking while keeping the final decision with a person.
2. Who is the intended user?
A distributor’s operations planner, retailer-service staff, or sales manager. The dashboard is an operator workspace rather than a consumer chatbot: it shows evidence, alternatives, exclusions, verification, cases, and policy citations.
3. Is this just a chat application?
No. Chat is only one intake surface. The main system is a decision workflow with contracts, business-state analysis, candidate planning, RAG citations, verification, durable retail review, evaluation, and human approval.
4. What is the main architectural principle?
Separate probabilistic language understanding from deterministic business authority. AI can interpret a message, but state supplies numbers, code calculates plans, a verifier checks them, and a human approves the review.
5. What did you personally work on?
“This was a team capstone. My work focused on the dashboard and integrated demonstration, scenario/evaluation presentation, integration checks, and validation of the complete workflow. I can explain how the team’s extraction, planning, RAG, retail, and evaluation modules connect through shared contracts.” Adjust this to match the final team record; do not claim sole ownership of teammates’ modules.

LLM and extraction

6. Where exactly is AI used?
The optional Qwen extractor classifies intent and extracts entities with evidence spans from free-form messages. It is behind the same interface as the deterministic rules extractor, so the rest of the workflow receives the same structured contract.
7. Why keep rules if an LLM is available?
Rules provide a no-key reproducible baseline, fast deterministic comparisons, and a fallback when model output fails or violates validation. The Evaluation page measures both rather than assuming the model is better.
8. Why not let the LLM choose the supplier?
Supplier selection requires exact stock, order, cost, capacity, date, and cash constraints. Deterministic code is auditable and repeatable, while an independent verifier can reconstruct the result. An LLM is not the authority for arithmetic or operational feasibility.
9. How do you prevent hallucinated facts?
I reduce rather than eliminate the risk. Output must satisfy a schema, evidence spans must be verbatim, catalogue references are checked, unknowns remain null, ambiguity triggers clarification, and invalid model output falls back to rules. The measured errors remain visible.
10. What happens when a revised delivery date is not stated?
It remains unknown. A two-day delay does not prove a revised date. The planner may calculate a scenario arrival from state and lead time, but labels that as a planning estimate, not a supplier commitment.
11. What happens when a product is ambiguous?
The system asks for clarification instead of guessing a SKU. The same principle appears in SCN-006: two matching orders mean the order reference is required before case review.
12. How do you handle prompt injection in a supplier message?
The message is treated as data, not instructions. The model must return a fixed extraction contract, and downstream stages only accept validated fields. Operational calculations and approval rules are deterministic and do not follow instructions embedded in message text.

RAG and knowledge

13. Why does this project need RAG?
A reviewer needs to know which policy, SOP, supplier term, or case rule supports a recommendation. RAG retrieves those passages with provenance so the reviewer can inspect the rule instead of trusting generated prose.
14. Does RAG provide stock, price, or cash values?
No. Versioned synthetic business state is the only source for those operational values. Retrieved documents provide policy and procedure context, and this separation prevents a document excerpt from becoming fake live state.
15. Why BM25 instead of vector search?
The current corpus is small, curated, and rich in exact policy terms. BM25 is local, deterministic, inexpensive, and measurable, and it outperformed the simple term-frequency baseline. Embeddings become more attractive for larger or multilingual semantic workloads.
16. What documents are included?
Thirty approved documents: 27 synthetic policies, SOPs, supplier terms, and case notes, plus three cited public regulatory extracts. The corpus register records source and provenance. The project does not claim that a large document count or file size equals usefulness.
17. How do citations remain trustworthy?
Quotes come verbatim from indexed sections, not generated summaries. Each reference includes document ID, effective date, and licence; retrieval filters documents by the scenario date. The evaluation checks all three SCN-003 references for these properties.
18. What do primary@1, recall@3, and MRR mean?
Primary@1 checks whether the canonical document is first. Recall@3 accepts the expected or labelled alternative in the first three. MRR rewards appearing earlier. Reporting all three avoids hiding weak first-rank performance behind perfect top-three recall.

Planning, verification, and scenarios

19. How is the recommended plan selected?
The planner creates stock-only and feasible supplier candidates, records exclusions, then sorts by unmet units, late units, scenario arrival, cost, and candidate ID. This lexicographic order is explicit and deterministic.
20. Why is the verifier called independent?
It does not trust the recommendation’s totals. It reconstructs allocations from original state and orders, then checks quantities, dates, offer terms, costs, cash, and constraint violations. A violation prevents a normal reviewable recommendation.
21. What is a scenario overlay?
An immutable “what-if” change such as 60% demand uplift, delayed receipt, or physical stock count. It is applied to a copy of the planning view without editing base synthetic CSV files. Multiple overlays can be composed for SCN-008.
22. What is the counterfactual in SCN-004?
The actual scenario has a delayed retailer receipt and cannot support immediate procurement, so 34 units remain unmet. A separate clearly labelled counterfactual asks what would happen if receipts were on time; it selects SUP-005. The system does not confuse that hypothetical with reality.
23. Is this a formal optimizer?
It is a deterministic candidate generator and lexicographic ranker, not a general mathematical optimization solver. That is appropriate for the transparent capstone scope. A future version could replace the ranking policy with a configurable or formal optimizer behind the same verifier.
24. Why does the system sometimes return an unmet plan?
Because a safe feasible purchase may not exist under cash or supplier constraints. The system exposes the shortfall rather than inventing money, supplier capacity, or stock. An unmet result can be the correct advisory outcome.

Retail workflows

25. How do you handle repeated competitor reports?
Duplicate deliveries are deduplicated, and corroboration counts distinct stable reporter IDs. Two reports from one person are not treated as independent confirmation. Even corroborated signals remain unverified and create no automatic pricing or assortment action.
26. What guards retailer reply drafts?
Drafts are deterministic acknowledgement templates. Staff edits are checked for unsupported commitments, money, dates, causes, substitutes, questions, and numbers. Approval is one-time and does not send or resolve the case; marking sent records a separate manual action.
27. Is the retail workflow durable?
Completed intake writes interactions, cases, drafts, and observations through one idempotent SQLite transaction keyed by event ID. Clarification rounds, duplicate deliveries, direct scenario runs, and failed writes are prevented from creating false persistence success.

Evaluation and engineering

28. What does “held out” mean?
The 12 extraction examples are reserved for measurement and excluded from prompts, rules tuning, examples, and retrieval-query construction. This reduces direct overfitting. They are still synthetic and too few for broad production claims.
29. Why is exact-record accuracy so low?
Exact-record is strict: intent and every labelled entity must be correct. The current rules and Qwen pipelines miss fields or classify some messages incorrectly. I show this because the purpose of evaluation is to locate improvement work, not to advertise the strongest single number.
30. Is twelve examples enough?
No, not for a production accuracy claim. It is enough to demonstrate a reproducible evaluation harness, progress visibility, validation/fallback inspection, and honest metric reporting. The next step is a larger independently labelled tuning set and a separate held-out set.
31. Do the results generalize to real distributor messages?
That has not been proven. All reported results are on synthetic data and self-written queries. Real evaluation requires authorized, de-identified examples and domain review, reported separately from synthetic measurements.
32. Which technologies did you use?
Python, FastAPI, Pydantic, LangGraph, Streamlit, SQLite, FTS5 BM25, exact Decimal arithmetic, an OpenAI-compatible Qwen endpoint, and pytest. The model is optional; deterministic scenarios remain runnable without it.
33. How do you know the integrated project works?
The verified build has 114 passing Python tests and 9/9 passing scenario evaluations. Browser checks covered live RAG and extraction progress, final metrics, drill-downs, fallback warnings, and the other dashboard pages. These verify the implemented demo, not real-world performance.
34. How are privacy and secrets handled?
The demo uses synthetic data. API keys stay in a gitignored environment file and are not committed or displayed. Real data would require permission, minimization, de-identification, restricted access, retention rules, and explicit authorization for model processing.
35. What would production require?
Authenticated channels and users, tenant isolation, transactional system-of-record integration, stronger audit and idempotency, monitored model quality, larger real-world evaluations, configurable business policy, and a separately authorized approved-action outbox. Operational execution remains outside this capstone.
36. What is the next most valuable improvement?
Improve extraction using a larger independently labelled tuning set while keeping the current held-out set untouched, then evaluate on a new held-out set. In parallel, add real reviewer-labelled retrieval questions. I would improve measured weak points rather than add more cosmetic AI features.
